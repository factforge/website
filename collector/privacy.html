SpotCollect Privacy Policy

Last updated November 3, 2026

Who we are

SpotCollect is made by Fact Forge LLC ("Fact Forge," "we," "our" or "us"). Its current test version is called Collector. SpotCollect turns a city into a collection: you find real places on the map, collect them when you're there and keep a journal of where you've been. This policy explains what SpotCollect collects, how we use it, who we share it with and how long we keep it. It covers SpotCollect on iPhone and on Android.

What we collect

  • No account. SpotCollect has no sign-in and doesn't ask for your name, email address or phone number.
  • Your journal stays on your phone. Your finds (which place, when, whether you were there and how far from it you were), stamps, levels and skills, trips, notes and tags, the places you add yourself with the location you give them, your photos and the hunts friends send you are stored only in the app on your phone. We don't have a copy. Your phone's own backup may include them: iCloud or your computer on iPhone, your Google account on Android.
  • Your location. SpotCollect uses your location on your phone while you use it: to show where you are on the map, to measure how far away each place is and to check that you're at a place when you collect it. Your location is never sent to us. A find keeps only how far you were from the place, not where you were. Nearby alerts are set up on your phone around the places' own locations, and your phone notices when you come close. On Android, turning nearby alerts on asks for your location all the time, so the alerts work while SpotCollect is closed.
  • Photos. You can add photos to your finds and to your own places, from the camera or your photo library. They stay in the app on your phone.
  • Usage data. SpotCollect uses Firebase Analytics to see how the app is used: when it opens, screens viewed, the city you choose, finds, stamps, levels and skills, backups, hunts made, received and solved, and suggestions sent. In the current test version (0.4, build 8), a find records which place it was (its ID in SpotCollect's list of places), its city and whether you were there, and a stamp records which stamp it was. Starting with the next version, a find records only the city and the kind and rarity of the place, and a stamp only what kind of stamp it is. These events come with Firebase's app-instance ID and basic device information, and Google works out your general location, such as your city or country, from your IP address. They never include your location, your notes, your photos, names or anything you type. SpotCollect doesn't use advertising identifiers.
  • Crash reports. Starting with the next version, Firebase Crashlytics sends us a report when SpotCollect crashes, with basic device information.
  • Places you suggest. Suggesting a place opens an email from your own mail app to factforge@atomicmail.io. It holds the place's name, kind, city and location, your note about it and its photo, saved again without location data. We receive it from your email address.
  • The places in the app. SpotCollect's list of places, with data from OpenStreetMap, is built into the app, so looking at places sends nothing anywhere.

How we use it

  • To run SpotCollect on your phone.
  • To find and fix problems, through crash reports, and to see how the app is used, through analytics.
  • To consider the places you suggest. A place we add may use the name, location and note you sent.
  • We do not sell or rent your personal data. SpotCollect shows no ads, has no advertising or ad-tracking code, and doesn't ask to track you.

Who we share it with

  • Google Firebase provides analytics (Firebase Analytics) and, starting with the next version, crash reporting (Crashlytics). Firebase encrypts data in transit with HTTPS. Google may store and process data wherever it has facilities. Firebase privacy and security
  • Apple distributes the app. On iPhone the map is Apple Maps, which receives the map area you're looking at. Apple's privacy policy
  • OpenFreeMap draws the map on Android. Its servers receive your IP address and the map area you're looking at, and OpenFreeMap says it doesn't store IP addresses. OpenFreeMap's privacy policy
  • Apple Maps or Google Maps open when you ask for directions, with the place's location as the destination. Google Privacy Policy
  • The people you choose. A hunt you share is a file with its title, riddle, spot and search radius, the name you add, and its photo, saved again without location data. It goes only where you send it, as do the recap and stamp pictures you share.

How long we keep it and how to delete it

  • Your journal stays on your phone until you delete it. You can uncollect a find, delete a place, a hunt or a photo, and reset your skills. Deleting SpotCollect deletes everything it stored on your phone. A journal backup you export is a file you keep or delete yourself.
  • Analytics events and crash reports aren't linked to a name or an account. Firebase Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days.
  • We keep the suggestion emails you send while we consider the place. Email factforge@atomicmail.io and we will delete yours.

Children

SpotCollect is not made for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes

We may update this policy from time to time. Changes will be posted on this page with an updated date.

Contact

Questions about this policy: factforge@atomicmail.io.
